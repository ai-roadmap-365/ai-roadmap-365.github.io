One AI endpoint -- 20,000 requests a month, 400ms each, 40 KB out -- priced
four ways at three traffic levels.

  launch (20k/mo)      serverless-free-tier $0.00    small-vm $7.79
  50x (1M/mo)          serverless-free-tier $10.14   small-vm $8.77
  1000x (20M/mo)       serverless-free-tier $295.88  small-vm $90.77

The cheapest option at launch is the most expensive one at scale, and the
crossover is not far away:

  serverless stops beating the VM at 45.0x -- 899,599 requests/month
  at that point serverless $8.66 vs vm $8.67

Nine hundred thousand requests a month is a small service. The option that is
free today is the wrong one before the service is meaningfully popular.

The number this lab exists for is the free-tier headroom table:

  egress-gb          1.25x current load
  storage-gb         2.50x current load
  compute-seconds   22.50x current load
  requests         100.00x current load

The tier advertises two million free requests -- a hundred times this traffic.
It ends at 1.25x, on egress, because the egress allowance is one gigabyte. The
advertised constraint and the binding constraint differ by a factor of eighty,
and only the second one decides when the bill starts.

That is the general shape of a free tier: generous on the dimension named in
the headline, tight on one that is not.

Two honest notes.

The demo reports "free until 1.30x" alongside "binds on egress-gb at 1.25x".
The gap is rounding -- a fraction of a gigabyte of egress costs under a cent and
displays as $0.00. The binding constraint is the number to trust.

The model prices a workload; it does NOT check that an option can serve one. At
the 1000x row it quotes a single 1 vCPU VM at $90.77 for twenty million
requests a month, which that machine could not deliver. This is stated in the
module docstring and in security.md rather than hidden, because a cost model
that silently answers the wrong question is worse than no cost model.
