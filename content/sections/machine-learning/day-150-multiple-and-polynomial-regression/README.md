# Day 150 — Multiple and Polynomial Regression

The tenth day of Course04 ("Machine Learning") and of Week 22 ("Regression").
It sits after Day 148, which owns the single-predictor line, and Day 149,
which owns why squared error is the loss, and before Day 151, which owns
ridge and lasso.

The through-line: **a coefficient in a multiple regression means "the
effect of this predictor, holding every other predictor constant" — and
that phrase stops describing anything trustworthy once two predictors are
correlated with each other, even while the model's predictions stay
completely fine.**

The lesson opens by duplicating a real predictor from the diabetes dataset
and watching its coefficient split into two arbitrary halves whose sum,
alone, is conserved. It then breaks that exact tie with one percent of
noise and watches the two halves swing across an eleven-unit range and
repeatedly change sign, while predictions move by fractions of a percent
and R-squared barely twitches. It computes variance inflation factors
directly from their definition, confirms with a bootstrap that high-VIF
predictors are the unstable ones, finds a genuine sign flip on real data,
proves a polynomial fit is linear in its parameters to thirteen decimal
places, and measures two quiet failures — R-squared never decreasing even
for pure noise, and scaling moving every coefficient without moving a
single prediction — that later days exist specifically to fix.

Scope boundaries, deliberately observed: Day 145 owns overfitting and the
capacity-versus-error trade-off (cited once, not re-taught); Day 148 owns
the single-predictor line and coefficient interpretation; Day 149 owns the
choice of squared-error loss and the normal equations; Day 151 owns ridge
and lasso, the standard cure for the instability measured here (cited in
one sentence, handed off); Day 152 owns adjusted R-squared, the fix for
the never-decreases problem measured here (cited, not implemented); Day
153 owns a from-scratch regression implementation; Day 154 is the week's
synthesis day.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel
with it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the
quiz from `quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source
list from `sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata
and time estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative
paths into `assets/`, and their alt text must stay identical to
`visuals.yml`. All cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days —
are generated by the layout from central configuration; never hard-code
repository or site URLs inside content files.

`duplicate-column-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the
file, so it animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a
still. Its five seed groups are revealed left to right so the instability
reads as motion, and it carries a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion:
reduce)` block that leaves every seed and every printed value visible at
once. Both SVGs prefix every class, id and keyframes name — `d150a-` in
the architecture diagram, `d150f-` in the flow diagram — because an
inlined SVG's `<style>` block is not scoped to that SVG and `url(#name)`
resolves document-wide, so two diagrams on one page would otherwise
restyle each other.

## Editing rules

- Every file must ship complete: no stub text, no unfinished sections,
  no filler — if a fact or figure is not verified, leave it out rather
  than approximating it.
- **Every number in this lesson is captured from a real run** on the
  authoring machine on 2026-08-27, using numpy 2.5.2, scikit-learn 1.9.0
  and pytest 9.1.1 in the lab's own lab-local virtual environment. If you
  change a number here, re-run the capture and change it in the lab's
  `expected-output/` directory too — check 6 of the harness compares the
  report byte for byte and will fail rather than let the lesson drift.
- **The duplicate-column contrast — wild coefficients, stable
  predictions — is the lesson's centrepiece and must never be presented
  with only one half of it.** Reporting the unstable coefficients without
  the conserved sum, or the conserved sum without the individually
  unstable coefficients, leaves out the argument.
- **Day 145's degree-sweep result (5.4911 at degree 4, 226,667.4689 at
  degree 24) is cited once, in one sentence, and never re-derived or
  re-taught.** This lesson's polynomial content is about mechanics
  (linear-in-parameters, design-matrix construction, interaction terms),
  not about the capacity-versus-error trade-off Day 145 already owns.
- **Ridge regression is named as the standard cure for multicollinearity
  in one sentence and handed off.** Do not explain, derive or demonstrate
  ridge or lasso in this lesson — that is Day 151's content in full.
- **Adjusted R-squared is named as the fix for the R2-never-decreases
  result in one sentence and handed off.** Do not implement or derive it
  here — that is Day 152's content.
- **`age`'s bootstrap coefficient of variation (4.70) is reported with its
  explanation, not silently omitted.** It is the largest in the dataset
  despite `age` having one of the lowest VIFs, because its mean
  coefficient sits near zero and inflates a ratio without reflecting
  genuine instability — the lab's own comparison excludes it for exactly
  this reason, and the lesson says so.
- Nested statsmodels VIF comparison, PCA-based remedies and manual feature
  selection are described from public documentation only. No output from
  any of them is reproduced anywhere in this lesson.
- **This lab needs no GPU** and must stay that way. It is small-array
  NumPy and scikit-learn on the CPU, and the lab README states so.
- Cite only sources listed in `sources.yml`; do not add URLs elsewhere in
  the content. All eight are free to read.
- When you revise any file in this directory, update `last_verified` in
  `lesson.yml` to the revision date.
- Keep image alt text in `index.mdx` and `visuals.yml` identical, and
  keep the exact H2 heading strings intact — the lesson validator checks
  them. Alt text must contain no square brackets: a closing bracket ends
  markdown image syntax and the alt renders broken.
- If you edit either SVG, keep every class, id and keyframes name behind
  its file's prefix (`d150a-` or `d150f-`), keep the
  `prefers-reduced-motion` block, and re-check with `xmllint --noout`.
- No LaTeX and no bare `{` or `<` in prose anywhere in `index.mdx` — MDX
  parses `{` and `}` as JSX and `<` as the start of a tag. Every code
  sample and every formula lives inside a fenced code block or backticks
  for exactly that reason; keep any addition the same way.
