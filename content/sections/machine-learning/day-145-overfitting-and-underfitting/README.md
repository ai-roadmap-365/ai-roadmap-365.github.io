# Day 145 — Overfitting and Underfitting

The fifth day of Course04 ("Machine Learning") and of Week 21 ("Machine
Learning Fundamentals"). It sits after Day 144, which owned splits, and
before Day 146, which owns the scikit-learn estimator API.

The through-line: **overfitting and underfitting are not two ends of one
dial. They are two different quantities — variance and bias — which
respond to completely different interventions**, and the lab measures both
directly rather than describing them.

The lesson opens on a model that scores better on data it has never seen
than on its own training set, a negative gap of −1.4942 that is the
signature of underfitting and is routinely mistaken for a broken split. It
then measures the decomposition over 200 independent training sets and
checks that bias squared plus variance plus noise equals the error
actually observed. It closes on the sentence the whole day exists for:
more data took an overfit model from 215,413 to the 4.0 floor and an
underfit one from 8.5023 to 8.2393.

Scope boundaries, deliberately observed: Day 141 owns what a score means;
Day 143 owns the workflow; Day 144 owns splits and **selection bias**,
which this day repeatedly distinguishes from overfitting because the two
are so often conflated; Day 146 owns the estimator API; Day 147 assembles
the week. This day uses `Pipeline`, `Ridge` and `SGDRegressor` without
teaching any of them.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel
with it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the
quiz from `quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source
list from `sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata
and time estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative
paths into `assets/`, and their alt text must stay identical to
`visuals.yml`. All cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days —
are generated by the layout from central configuration; never hard-code
repository or site URLs inside content files.

`capacity-curve-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the
file, so it animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a
still. Its marker pairs are revealed left to right so the divergence of
the two curves reads as motion, and it carries a
`@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that leaves both curves
and all twenty markers in place. Both SVGs prefix every class, id and
keyframes name — `d145a-` in the architecture diagram, `d145f-` in the
flow diagram — because an inlined SVG's `<style>` block is not scoped to
that SVG and `url(#name)` resolves document-wide, so two diagrams on one
page would otherwise restyle each other.

## Editing rules

- Every file must ship complete: no stub text, no unfinished sections,
  no filler — if a fact or figure is not verified, leave it out rather
  than approximating it.
- **Every number in this lesson is captured from a real run** on the
  authoring machine on 2026-08-27, using numpy 2.5.2, scikit-learn 1.9.0
  and pytest 9.1.1 in the lab's own lab-local virtual environment. If you
  change a number here, re-run the capture and change it in the lab's
  `expected-output/` directory too — check 6 of the harness compares the
  report byte for byte and will fail rather than let the lesson drift.
- **The negative gap is a headline, not a footnote.** −1.4942 at degree 1
  is the signature of underfitting and the finding most often mistaken for
  a broken split. It opens the lesson and it must keep doing so.
- **The training curve is not monotone and the lesson says so.** It
  wobbles upward by 0.0636 past degree 14 because 25 rows meet exactly 25
  features. That is floating point, not learning theory, and the lab
  asserts monotonicity through degree 14 while asserting that it fails
  overall. Do not present a clean curve.
- **The interpolation threshold was found by accident and is kept.** The
  degree-24 column is worse at n=25 than at n=15. The lab proves the
  mechanism with a feature count rather than asserting it, and the lesson
  names it as the left-hand edge of double descent without over-claiming.
- **Degree 2 having more bias than degree 1 is stated as a measurement
  with a mechanism, not as a theorem.** In population terms a larger model
  class cannot have more bias; what is measured is the average over 200
  finite fits. The lesson and the quiz both make that distinction, and it
  must not be flattened into "bigger classes can have more bias".
- **The decomposition's tolerances are honest and must stay so.** The
  predicted total agrees with the observed error to within 1.003 percent
  at worst — that gap is Monte Carlo error in the check, not error in the
  identity — and the three parts sum to the total within 0.0002 because
  each is stored already rounded. Do not tighten either to look neater.
- **The early-stopping rule is not endorsed.** Every patience from 5 to 50
  recovers epoch 14 on this run, and so does stop-at-first-increase, but
  the lab records that this is luck rather than a property of the rule
  because the test curve wanders. Keep that caveat.
- **Overfitting and selection bias must stay distinguished.** They are
  routinely conflated and the confusion is expensive: this lesson's
  226,667 has nothing to do with how many models were compared, and Day
  144's 0.5720 has nothing to do with capacity.
- Bagging, boosting and managed tuning services are described from public
  documentation only. No output from any is reproduced, the Alternatives
  section says so, and **no price is quoted** for the paid options.
- **This lab needs no GPU** and must stay that way. It is small
  least-squares solves on the CPU, and the lab README states so.
- Cite only sources listed in `sources.yml`; do not add URLs elsewhere in
  the content. All nine are free to read.
- When you revise any file in this directory, update `last_verified` in
  `lesson.yml` to the revision date.
- Keep image alt text in `index.mdx` and `visuals.yml` identical, and
  keep the exact H2 heading strings intact — the lesson validator checks
  them. Alt text must contain no square brackets: a closing bracket ends
  markdown image syntax and the alt renders broken.
- If you edit either SVG, keep every class, id and keyframes name behind
  its file's prefix (`d145a-` or `d145f-`), keep the
  `prefers-reduced-motion` block, and re-check with `xmllint --noout`.
- No LaTeX and no bare `{` or `<` in prose anywhere in `index.mdx` — MDX
  parses `{` and `}` as JSX and `<` as the start of a tag. Every code
  sample and every formula lives inside a fenced code block or backticks
  for exactly that reason; keep any addition the same way.
