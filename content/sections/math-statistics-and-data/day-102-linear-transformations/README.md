# Day 102 — Linear Transformations

The fourth lesson of Week 15 ("Linear Algebra I: Vectors and Matrices"), and
the day the "transformation" reading of a matrix — promised on Day 100 and
given its arithmetic on Day 101 — gets its full treatment.

The organising idea, stated in the first section and returned to in every
section after it:

> **A matrix is a function, and its columns are where the basis vectors land.**

If you know where `(1, 0)` and `(0, 1)` go, you know where everything goes,
because every vector is a combination of those two and a linear transformation
preserves combinations. Nothing else in the lesson is independent of that
sentence.

The lesson opens by breaking something, in the pattern this course uses for its
strongest days. Four points arranged in the exclusive-or pattern cannot be
separated by a straight line, so the reader stacks twenty matrix layers — and
the lesson shows, from a real captured run, that the twenty collapse into a
single 2×2 matrix. Depth bought nothing. That failure is the spine of the day:
it reappears in the linearity tests, in the composition section, in the quiz,
in the lab's sixth reference script, and in the closing AI thread, and it is
the honest, measurable reason activation functions exist.

The rest is the mechanics, each landed against real captured output: reading a
matrix off a described picture and reading the picture back off the columns,
with the row-is-not-a-landing trap stated explicitly; the two conditions that
define *linear*, tested where they hold and where they fail, with the failure
quantified as exactly `b` and exactly `(s − 1) · b`; scaling, reflection, shear
and rotation all **derived** rather than given, with the rotation matrix coming
out of the unit circle and cosine, sine and the radian defined from scratch for
a reader who has not met them; composition as multiplication with the order
gotcha tied back to Day 101; the determinant introduced as a **measured area**
with the negative and zero cases both demonstrated; the inverse and when it
exists; rank in plain language; and the identity as the do-nothing case.

Two floating-point facts are asserted rather than hidden, because both would
otherwise look like bugs: `cos(π / 2)` is `6.123233995736766e-17` and not
`0.0`, which is why every comparison in the lab states a tolerance; and
`numpy.linalg.det` on `[[3, -1], [1, 2]]` returns `7.000000000000001` where the
direct `a*d − b*c` returns exactly `7.0`, because NumPy factorises the matrix
using the general routine that also works at 500×500 and that factorisation
rounds.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel with
it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the quiz from
`quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source list from
`sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata and time
estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative paths into
`assets/`, and their alt text must stay identical to `visuals.yml`. All
cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days — are generated by the layout
from central configuration; never hard-code repository or site URLs inside
content files.

`transformation-compose-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the
file, so it animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a still.
It carries a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that disables every
animation and restores the resting state, and the still frame carries the whole
meaning on its own — every matrix, coordinate and caption is present without
motion. Both SVGs prefix every class, id and keyframes name — `d102a-` in the
architecture diagram, `d102f-` in the flow diagram — because an inlined SVG's
`<style>` block is not scoped to that SVG and `url(#name)` resolves
document-wide, so two diagrams on one page would otherwise restyle each other.

  anyone, because the cited pages carry no such attribution. Do not add names
