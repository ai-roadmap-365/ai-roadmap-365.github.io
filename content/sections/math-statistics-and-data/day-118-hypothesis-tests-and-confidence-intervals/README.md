# Day 118 — Hypothesis Tests and Confidence Intervals

The sixth lesson of Week 17 ("Probability and Statistics"), directly after
Day 117's sampling distribution and standard error. Week 17 closes in an
A/B Test Analyzer on Day 119; today builds the testing and interval
machinery Day 119 applies to a single real experiment end to end.

The organising sentence, returned to throughout:

> **A p-value answers a far narrower question than almost everyone
> thinks, and a confidence interval is usually the better thing to
> report.**

## The opening failure

A team checks twenty metrics before shipping a change. Nineteen come back
unremarkable; one comes back p = 0.031, and they write it up as a real
finding. Nobody cheated — but with twenty independent tests at alpha =
0.05, the probability of at least one false positive purely from noise is
`1 - 0.95^20 = 0.6415`, a 64% chance, not a fluke. This lesson derives
that number by hand, confirms it by simulation, and shows Bonferroni
pulling the family-wise rate back to about 4.9%.

## What the lesson covers, each with a measured number

- The two-sample z-test, built from `math.erf` alone, checked against an
  independent hand computation to nine decimal places.
- **The centrepiece: coverage.** 10,000 nominal-95% confidence intervals
  built from a population with a known true mean; measured coverage
  `9,509/10,000 = 95.09%`, within three standard errors of the nominal
  95% — the actual content of "95% confidence," measured rather than
  recited.
- **The duality**, checked with zero exceptions: across 2,000 simulated
  datasets, a two-sided test at alpha rejected the null exactly when the
  matching (1 - alpha) interval excluded it — every single time.
- The permutation test, built from scratch with no distributional
  assumption: agrees closely with the z-test at a moderate n from a
  normal population (`|difference| = 0.0070`) and diverges more at a
  small n from a skewed population (`|difference| = 0.0981`), where the
  normal approximation is on shakier ground.
- **Multiple comparisons — the opening claim, confirmed.** Exact
  `1 - 0.95^20 = 0.6415`, simulated at `0.6435`; Bonferroni-corrected
  analytic target `0.0488`, simulated at `0.0515`.
- Statistical power, rising monotonically with both `n` and effect size;
  a closed-form formula (`0.3444` at effect=2.8, n=100) agreeing with a
  direct 3,000-trial simulation (`0.3460`) to within `0.0016`.
- Effect size versus significance: the identical `0.5%` relative
  difference is not significant at n=30 (`p=0.5675`) and is significant
  at n=100,000 (`p` rounds to `0.000000`), with the effect size itself
  unchanged.
- **Peeking**, measured directly: checking a true null after every 10
  observations and stopping at the first p<0.05 gives a false-positive
  rate of `0.1888` — nearly 4x the nominal `0.05` a single, fixed-n test
  achieved (`0.0493`).
- The bootstrap interval versus the normal-approximation interval,
  agreeing to within `0.008` standard errors on their centers and `3%` on
  their widths.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel
with it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the
quiz from `quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source
list from `sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata
and time estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative
paths into `assets/`, and their alt text stays identical to `visuals.yml`.
All cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days — are generated by
the layout from central configuration; never hard-code repository or site
URLs inside content files.

`ci-coverage-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the file,
so it animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a still.
It carries a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that disables
every animation and restores the resting state, and the still frame
carries the whole meaning on its own — all twenty interval bars, the
amber miss, the true-value line and both captions are present without
motion. Both SVGs prefix every class, id and keyframes name — `d118a-` in
the architecture diagram, `d118f-` in the flow diagram, including the
twenty per-row stagger classes (`d118f-r0` through `d118f-r19`) — because
an inlined SVG's `<style>` block is not scoped to that SVG and
`url(#name)` resolves document-wide, so two diagrams on one page would
otherwise restyle each other.

No LaTeX appears anywhere in `index.mdx`. Every formula is written in
inline backticks or plain prose, because MDX parses `{` and `}` as JSX and
a `$$…$$` block broke the site build on Day 109.
