# Day 113 — Probability: Events, Rules, and Intuition

The first lesson of Week 17 ("Probability and Statistics"), and the first
probability content in the entire course, arriving directly after two weeks
of linear algebra and calculus. Week 17 ends in an A/B Test Analyzer on Day
119; today lays the foundations everything through Day 118's hypothesis
tests is built on.

The organising sentence, returned to throughout:

> **Probability is bookkeeping about sets, and almost every "paradox" is a
> bookkeeping error you can find. When intuition and arithmetic disagree,
> enumerate — and when the space is too large to enumerate, simulate.**

## The opening failure

The lesson opens with the Chevalier de Méré's 1650s paradox: betting on at
least one 6 in 4 rolls of one die felt, to an experienced and sophisticated
gambler, exactly as good as betting on at least one double-six in 24 rolls
of two dice — a double six being 1/6 as likely, so 6x the rolls should even
things out. It does not: `1 - (5/6)^4 = 0.5177...` is favourable, `1 -
(35/36)^24 = 0.4914...` is not, and the correspondence between Pascal and
Fermat that resolved the gap is usually credited as founding the
mathematical theory of probability. The lesson derives both numbers exactly
with the complement rule in one line each, then confirms both by
simulation — the derive-then-check pattern that structures the entire day.

## What the lesson covers, each with a worked number

- Sample space and events as sets, probability as `|event| / |space|` via
  `fractions.Fraction` — no floating-point noise, ever.
- The addition rule, with `A = "sum is 7"` and `B = "first die is 6"`
  showing the naive sum overstating the truth by exactly `P(A and B) =
  1/36`.
- The complement rule as the one-line resolution of de Méré's paradox.
- Independence versus mutual exclusivity, with a table and two worked
  examples proving mutually exclusive events with non-zero probability are
  *necessarily* dependent.
- Conditional probability as restriction — filtering the sample space down
  to the condition and asking the question again inside the smaller table,
  checked exactly against the formula on `P(sum = 8 | first die is even) =
  1/6`.
- The multiplication rule and the chain rule for probability, with an
  explicit, honest note that it shares nothing with Day 110's chain rule
  beyond a structural echo and the name.
- The law of total probability over two urns, set up carefully because Day
  115's Bayes' theorem runs this exact computation backwards.
- Monte Carlo error scaling: measured, not asserted, that error shrinks as
  `1/sqrt(n)` rather than `1/n` — about 23.5x shrink from n=100 to
  n=100,000 against a `sqrt(1000) ≈ 31.6x` prediction.
- The four traps: the gambler's fallacy, `P(A|B)` versus `P(B|A)`, base-rate
  neglect, and the conjunction fallacy — each with a worked or measured
  example rather than a bare warning.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel with
it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the quiz from
`quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source list from
`sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata and time
estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative paths into
`assets/`, and their alt text stays identical to `visuals.yml`. All
cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days — are generated by the
layout from central configuration; never hard-code repository or site URLs
inside content files.

`conditioning-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the file,
so it animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a still. It
carries a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that disables
every animation and restores the resting state, and the still frame carries
the whole meaning on its own — all three panels, all 36+18+18 dots, both
formula routes and every caption are present without motion. Both SVGs
prefix every class, id and keyframes name — `d113a-` in the architecture
diagram, `d113f-` in the flow diagram — because an inlined SVG's `<style>`
block is not scoped to that SVG and `url(#name)` resolves document-wide, so
two diagrams on one page would otherwise restyle each other.

No LaTeX appears anywhere in `index.mdx`. Every formula is written in a
fenced ` ```text ` block or inline backticks, because MDX parses `{` and `}`
as JSX and a `$$…$$` block broke the site build on Day 109.
