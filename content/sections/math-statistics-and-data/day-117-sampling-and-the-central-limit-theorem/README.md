# Day 117 — Sampling and the Central Limit Theorem

The fifth lesson of Week 17 ("Probability and Statistics"), directly after
Day 116's descriptive statistics. Week 17 closes in an A/B Test Analyzer on
Day 119; today builds the sampling-distribution and standard-error
machinery Days 118 and 119 lean on directly.

The organising sentence, returned to throughout:

> **The central limit theorem is the reason a sample can tell you anything
> about a population, and the standard error is the price you pay for
> asking.**

## The opening failure

Two samples of 30 users, drawn from the exact same population, give
average session lengths of 42 seconds and 51 seconds. Nothing was done
incorrectly in either measurement — a sample statistic is itself a random
variable, with its own distribution, and reporting one draw from it as
"the answer" without its spread is the mistake this lesson exists to
prevent.

## What the lesson covers, each with a measured number

- The sampling distribution built literally, by repeating a
  draw-and-average process 20,000 times: its own mean lands within half a
  standard error of the truth, and its own spread lands within a tenth of
  a standard error of `sigma / sqrt(n)`.
- The `sqrt(n)` law, measured across four sample sizes each 4x the last:
  ratios of `1.985`, `1.991` and `2.011` against a predicted `2.0`.
- The central limit theorem, measured as the skewness of a sampling
  distribution built from a heavily skewed population falling
  monotonically from `1.42` at n=2 to `0.11` at n=320.
- **The Cauchy counterexample — the heart of the lesson.** An Exponential
  population's sample mean tightens `9.85x` from n=10 to n=1,000; a
  Cauchy population's sample mean tightens by only `1.02x` — essentially
  not at all — because the Cauchy distribution has no defined mean or
  variance.
- **Sampling bias versus sampling error.** A sampler restricted to the
  upper half of a population shows a mean absolute error of `2.0712` at
  n=30 and `2.0722` at n=3,000 — unchanged across a hundredfold growth in
  n — while an honest sampler's error shrinks `10.28x` over the same
  range.
- The bootstrap, built from scratch: agrees with `sigma_hat / sqrt(n)` for
  the mean within about 1%, then applied unchanged to the median, where no
  formula exists.
- Dependence quietly inflating the true standard error: an AR(1) series'
  true standard error (`0.1692`, by replication) versus the naive formula
  (`0.0693`) — a factor of `2.44` understatement.
- The evaluation-margin calculation: a 0.3-point accuracy gap on a
  500-example test set sits at only `0.24` standard errors — noise, not
  evidence.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel
with it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the
quiz from `quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source
list from `sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata
and time estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative
paths into `assets/`, and their alt text stays identical to `visuals.yml`.
All cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days — are generated by
the layout from central configuration; never hard-code repository or site
URLs inside content files.

`clt-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the file, so it
animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a still. It
carries a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that disables
every animation and restores the resting state, and the still frame
carries the whole meaning on its own — all four stages of both panels,
their labels and both closing captions are present without motion. Both
SVGs prefix every class, id and keyframes name — `d117a-` in the
architecture diagram, `d117f-` in the flow diagram — because an inlined
SVG's `<style>` block is not scoped to that SVG and `url(#name)` resolves
document-wide, so two diagrams on one page would otherwise restyle each
other.

No LaTeX appears anywhere in `index.mdx`. Every formula is written in
inline backticks or plain prose, because MDX parses `{` and `}` as JSX and
a `$$…$$` block broke the site build on Day 109.
