# Day 103 — Dot Products and Similarity

The fifth lesson of Week 15 ("Linear Algebra I: Vectors and Matrices"), and the
day the week's arithmetic starts paying for itself. Day 99 introduced vectors
and Euclidean distance; Days 100 to 102 built matrices, multiplication and
linear transformations. This day goes back to Day 99's six-article table,
unchanged, and asks it a better question.

The through-line, stated in the first section and returned to in every section
after it: **Euclidean distance answers "how far apart are these", and that is
often the wrong question. Cosine similarity answers "do these point the same
way", and for text and embeddings that is usually the right one.**

The lesson opens by breaking Day 99's own result. Take the roast-chicken
article and write it again at twice the length — every count doubled, same
subject, same emphasis. Euclidean distance puts the doubled copy **9.0554**
away from the original, while race-day-nutrition, an article mostly about
running, sits at **8.0623**. The measure says an article's own doubled copy is
further away than a genuinely different article is. That failure is computed,
not asserted, and it is the day's spine: it reappears in the search section, in
the ranking-equivalence proof, in the quiz, and in the lab.

The rest is the mechanics, each landed against real captured output: the dot
product's geometric meaning and the projection picture; the sign of the dot
product read as an angle, with one worked example of each case; cosine
similarity as the dot product of the two unit vectors, and therefore
magnitude-free by construction; cosine distance and the honest admission that
it is not a metric, with a concrete triple where the triangle inequality
fails; the identity that makes both measures rank normalised vectors
identically, which is why vector stores normalise on the way in; orthogonality
as "unrelated rather than opposed"; when Euclidean distance is genuinely the
right choice; and a measured treatment of the curse of dimensionality.

The from-scratch build is dot, cosine similarity and cosine distance in pure
Python, asserted against NumPy, and then a working semantic search on top of
them in four lines.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel with
it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the quiz from
`quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source list from
`sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata and time
estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative paths into
`assets/`, and their alt text must stay identical to `visuals.yml`. All
cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days — are generated by the layout
from central configuration; never hard-code repository or site URLs inside
content files.

`cosine-vs-euclidean-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the
file, so it animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a still.
It carries a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that disables every
animation and restores the resting state, and the still frame carries the whole
meaning on its own — every number, label and line of arithmetic is present
without motion. Both SVGs prefix every class, id and keyframes name —
`d103a-` in the architecture diagram, `d103f-` in the flow diagram — because an
inlined SVG's `<style>` block is not scoped to that SVG and `url(#name)`
resolves document-wide, so two diagrams on one page would otherwise restyle each
other. Neither file uses a bare element selector for the same reason; the shared
font is set as a presentation attribute on the root `<svg>` instead.

## Editing rules

- Every file must ship complete: no stub text, no unfinished sections, no

  approximating it.
- **Every block of output shown in this lesson was captured from a real run on
  the authoring machine on 2026-08-16**, with Python 3.14.0, numpy 2.5.2 and
  pytest 9.1.1 on macOS 26.5.2 (Apple Silicon). The captures live in the lab's
  `expected-output/` directory. If you change a number here, re-run the lab and
  change it there too, and keep the indentation identical — these are quotes,
  not paraphrases.
- The six-article table is Day 99's, unchanged, and must stay unchanged. The
  entire argument of the day is that one dataset answers two different
  questions, and that argument evaporates if the data is quietly improved.
- The opening failure — 9.0554 against 8.0623 — is the load-bearing claim.
  Do not soften it into a caution, and do not move it out of the opening
  section; the lesson is built to start by breaking something.
- The clamping discussion reports a real finding: three of the six articles
  miss exact 1.0 when compared with themselves through the unguarded formula,
  and `race-day-nutrition` returns `1.0000000000000002`, which `math.acos`
  refuses. This was found by the reference suite failing during authoring, not
  anticipated. Keep it attributed that way.
- The dimensionality section reports the **exact** mean of the absolute cosine,
  `gamma(d/2) / (sqrt(pi) gamma((d+1)/2))`, alongside the commonly quoted
  approximation `sqrt(2 / (pi d))`. The approximation is a large-`d` limit and
  is wrong at `d = 2` and `d = 3`, where the exact values `2/pi` and `0.5` are
  hand-checkable. The measurement agrees with the exact column. Do not replace
  the exact column with the approximation.
- The claim that the angle is a metric while `1 - cos` is not is stated as
  known and explicitly **not proved here**; the lesson checks it on one triple
  and says that one triple is not a proof. Keep that hedge.
- NumPy 2.5.2 is the only library anything was run on. `scipy.spatial.distance`
  is described from its documentation and is explicitly marked as not
  installed, with no output reproduced. Keep that marking; it is the difference
  between a description and a claim.
- The lesson names cosine, dot product and Euclidean distance as the similarity
  options vector stores commonly expose, and deliberately invents no detail
  about any specific product the course has not reached. Do not add product
  names, versions, prices or tier limits.
- All data is invented and is stated to be invented — the six articles, the
  four features and every count. Keep any replacement equally obviously
  fictional, and keep at least one pair exactly orthogonal, because the
  orthogonality section depends on it.
- Cite only sources listed in `sources.yml`; do not add URLs elsewhere in the
  content.
- When you revise any file in this directory, update `last_verified` in
  `lesson.yml` to the revision date.
- Keep image alt text in `index.mdx` and `visuals.yml` identical, and keep the
  exact H2 heading strings intact — the lesson validator checks them. Alt text
  must contain no square brackets: a closing bracket ends markdown image syntax
  and the alt renders broken. This is why the diagram alts write "a with
  components 3 and 4" rather than the bracketed vectors used everywhere else.
- If you edit either SVG, keep every class, id and keyframes name behind its
