# Day 066 — Exceptions and Error Handling Strategy

The third lesson of Week 10 ("Files, Errors, and Object-Oriented Python").
Day 64 moved bytes in and out of files and Day 65 gave those bytes an agreed
structure; both generated failures neither day had a policy for. This lesson
supplies the policy. It treats error handling as a design problem rather than
a syntax feature: return codes versus raised exceptions and each strategy's
failure mode, what the interpreter actually does when a raise unwinds the
stack, how to read a real traceback, the full try/except/else/finally
anatomy, the built-in hierarchy and why KeyboardInterrupt sits outside
Exception, chaining with `raise ... from err`, EAFP versus LBYL and the race
condition behind it, the placement strategy (fail fast at the boundary,
propagate through the middle, handle where you can act), `logging.exception`
versus print, why `assert` is not validation, and a from-scratch `@retry`
decorator with exponential backoff. The sustained analogy is a hospital
triage system: who handles what, at which level, and what gets escalated.
