# Day 081 — Scheduling and Background Jobs

The fourth lesson of Week 12 ("Python for Automation and the Web"), and the
day the week's output stops being something you run and becomes something that
runs itself. Days 78 to 80 produced a fetcher, a scraper and a command-line
tool — three things obviously worth running every night. Today is about
running them unattended, and about the fact that the unattended part, not the
scheduling part, is where the difficulty lives.

The lesson is deliberately lopsided. About a fifth of it covers the schedulers
themselves: sleep loops and their drift, `sched` and `threading.Timer`, cron's
five fields including the day-of-month/day-of-week OR rule, a launchd plist
field by field, and a systemd `.service`/`.timer` pair with an honest account
of why timers are nicer than cron. The remaining four fifths cover the
engineering that decides whether a scheduled job can be trusted: idempotence
and atomic writes, catch-up and backfill, overlapping runs and `flock`,
timeouts and process groups, time zones and daylight saving, structured
logging, meaningful exit codes, and — the sentence the day is built around —
alerting on the absence of a success rather than the presence of a failure,
because a job that stops running produces no error at all.

The whole thing rests on Day 74. Because the clock arrives as a parameter,
every claim in the lesson is a test that runs in milliseconds: the drift of a
hundred iterations, a six-hour `sched` schedule, an eleven-day-stale watchdog,
and what a 02:30 job does on the morning the clocks change.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel with
it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the quiz from
`quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source list from
`sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata, and time
estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative paths into
`assets/`, and their alt text must stay in sync with `visuals.yml`. All
cross-links (to the lab, to neighboring days) are generated by the layout
from central configuration — never hard-code repository or site URLs inside
content files.

## Editing rules

- Every file must ship complete: no stub text, no unfinished sections, no filler of any kind — if a fact or figure is not verified, leave it out rather than approximating it.
- **The safety rule is not editorial preference.** The lab must never install anything into a learner's real crontab, launchd or systemd, and must never leave a background process running. If you change the lab, keep section 8 of `tests/run_tests.sh` intact — it asserts exactly that, and it is the reason this day is safe to ship.
- Every code output shown in the lesson is captured from a real run on the authoring machine (macOS 26.5.1, Python 3.14.0, pytest 9.1.1, bash 3.2.57, 2026-07-19). If the lab code changes, re-run it and replace the captured text rather than editing the numbers by hand.
- The `schedule`, APScheduler, `croniter` and Celery snippets in the Alternatives section are written from those projects' documented interfaces and are explicitly labelled as **not installed here**. Keep that labelling; do not present them as captured output. No price is quoted for any managed service, deliberately — pricing changes and a stale figure is worse than none.
- The daylight-saving dates (2026-03-08 and 2026-11-01, `America/New_York`) come from the operating system's IANA time zone database via `zoneinfo`, and the lab asserts them. If you change the zone or the dates, re-run `pytest examples -q -k timezone` rather than editing the prose.
- Cite only sources listed in `sources.yml`; do not add URLs elsewhere in the content.
- When you revise any file in this directory, update `last_verified` in `lesson.yml` to the revision date.
- Keep image alt text in `index.mdx` and `visuals.yml` identical, keep square brackets out of alt text entirely, and keep the exact H2 heading strings intact — the lesson validator checks all three.
- Regenerate any downstream exports (search index, print/PDF builds) after edits so rendered outputs match the source.
