# Day 095 — Dates, Times, and Time Zones

The fourth lesson of Week 14 ("Data Formats and Pipelines"), and the one every
other day in the week quietly depends on. Days 92 to 94 covered key-value
stores, ORMs and validation; this day covers the field that appears in every
schema, every log line and every training set, and that almost nobody stores
correctly the first time.

The through-line, and it is stated in the first screen of the lesson:
**a timestamp without a time zone is not a time, it is a rumour.** The lesson
opens by breaking something concrete — a nightly job that charged every
customer twice on 25 October 2026 and did not run at all on 29 March 2026,
from one correct-looking schedule entry — and shows the bytes that caused it.

The sustained analogy is **the tape measure and the labels**: real time is a
tape being pulled out at a constant rate, a local clock is a set of labels
stuck onto it, and twice a year somebody re-labels a stretch while it is still
moving — duplicating a set of labels in October and skipping one in March.
Every concept in the day maps onto that picture without strain, including the
two that usually confuse people: `fold` (which of two identical labels you
meant) and `time.monotonic()` (a stopwatch that measures tape and ignores
labels entirely).

The depth requirement is met by a genuine from-scratch build. The lesson
implements a zone-offset resolver over a three-line transition table, in both
directions — instant to offset, which always has exactly one answer, and wall
clock to offset, which has one, two or none — and then checks it against the
real IANA database on thirteen wall readings and both values of `fold`,
twenty-six comparisons, zero disagreements. After that, `zoneinfo` is no
longer magic; it is thirty lines the reader has already written.

Everything quoted in the lesson is captured from real runs on the authoring
machine on 2026-08-16: the 23-hour and 25-hour days, the two epoch seconds
3600 apart, the round trip that does not come home, the three sorts, the
negative duration a wall-clock stopwatch reports, and the resolver's agreement
table.

## How this lesson is rendered

`index.mdx` provides the lesson body, and the sidecar YAML files travel with
it: the site layout renders the body at the day route, injects the quiz from
`quiz.yml`, the glossary from `glossary.yml`, and the source list from
`sources.yml`, and uses `lesson.yml` for navigation, metadata and time
estimates. Images are referenced from `index.mdx` with relative paths into
`assets/`, and their alt text must stay identical to `visuals.yml`. All
cross-links — to the lab and to neighbouring days — are generated by the
layout from central configuration; never hard-code repository or site URLs
inside content files.

`dst-transition-flow.svg` is animated with CSS `@keyframes` inside the file,
so it animates wherever it is rendered as an image and prints as a still. It
carries a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that disables every
animation and restores the resting state, and the still frame carries the
whole meaning on its own — every instant, offset, epoch value and label is
present without motion. Both SVGs prefix every class, id and keyframes name —
`d095a-` in the architecture diagram, `d095f-` in the flow diagram — because
an inlined SVG's `<style>` block is not scoped to that SVG and `url(#name)`
resolves document-wide, so two diagrams on one page would otherwise restyle
each other.

  authoring machine on 2026-08-16, offline, using the standard library only.
  The captures live in the lab's `expected-output/` directory; if you change a
  number here, re-run the lab and change it there too.
- The environment facts are specific and were observed, not assumed: Python
  3.14.0, IANA time zone database **2026c** at `/usr/share/zoneinfo`, **598**
  zones available. The zone count and the database version are the two values
  a reader's machine may legitimately differ on, and the lesson says so.
  Re-verify before changing any of them.
- The 2026 transition instants — 2026-03-29T01:00Z and 2026-10-25T01:00Z for
  Europe/London — are load-bearing. Every worked example, every assertion and
  both diagrams are built on them. They came from the database on this
  machine, not from memory.
- Nothing in the lesson or the lab may read the clock for a value it asserts
  on. Every instant is pinned in the source, because a test that uses "now"
  cannot be trusted on the one day of the year this material is about. The
  lab's test suite checks this.
- The comparison trap — two aware datetimes an hour apart returning `True`
  from `==` — is documented behaviour, not a bug, and the lesson says so while
  still treating it as a hazard. Do not soften it into a warning and do not
  present it as a defect.
- Only `zoneinfo`, `datetime`, `time` and `calendar` were run here. `pytz`,
  `dateutil`, `arrow` and `pendulum` are described from their documentation
  and **no output is reproduced for any of them**. The Alternatives section
  states this explicitly; keep that statement if you edit around it.
